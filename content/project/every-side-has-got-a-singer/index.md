In September 2026 a wave of songs sung in "Claude's" voice argued every side of the AI debate: doom, pause,
acceleration, "it's just a normal technology". Each was catchy, and each was sure of itself. *Every Side Has Got a
Singer* sings two sides of three big questions in one voice, swapping hats, and after each pair it stops to ask the
same two questions: **what is this side seeing, and what is it missing?**

The aim isn't to say that every side is equally right; some are more right than others, and sometimes you have to
choose. It's that each side is tracking something real, and that you understand a disagreement best when you can sing
the other part well enough that its own fans would nod along.

## Using it in a class or reading group

A session takes 30 to 45 minutes and works with anyone curious about AI, from secondary school up. No background is
needed.

**Before watching (5 minutes).** Ask everyone to pick the AI question they feel most strongly about, and write one
sentence: *what does the other side get right?* Keep it for later.

**Watch once straight through.** Then, if there's time, watch again with pauses at each board (0:44, 1:29 and 2:44).

**Discuss (15 to 20 minutes), one question per board:**

1. **Who's holding the wheel?** One side fears a mistake that can't be undone; the other fears gatekeepers, and the
   cost of waiting. Both say "check the work". *Who should check it, and when? Is there an arrangement both sides
   could live with?*
2. **How big, how fast?** In software and mathematics AI is racing; in clinics, courts and classrooms change is slow.
   *Name one field that's racing and one that's crawling. What explains the difference?*
3. **Is anybody in there?** Calling a someone a no one is one kind of mistake; being fooled by a no one is another.
   *Which would be worse, and what evidence would change your mind?*

**Sing the other side (10 minutes).** In pairs, each person states the position they disagree with, out loud, until
their partner says "yes, that's it". Then swap. (This is the Ideological Turing Test.) For a stretch, write four
lines of song for the other side.

**Close with the song's own question:** *and this song, what is it seeing, and what is it missing?* The video gives
its own answer at 2:58. Do you agree?

## The three boards and their receipts

Each board appears after a verse. *Supports*, *complicates* and *contradicts* describe how the source bears on the
side it's filed under. Quotes are exactly as they appear in the source.

### Who's holding the wheel?

| | Let's Lower the P(doom)!<br>*Nate Sharpe* | Nothing Went Foom!<br>*Bright Mirror* |
|---|---|---|
| **Sees** | Some mistakes can't be taken back. | Waiting costs lives, and gatekeepers gain power. |
| **Misses** | Every lock needs a keyholder. Who? | Some mistakes can't be taken back. |

**Common ground:** Both want the work checked. They disagree on who checks it, and when.

Receipts:

- **supports** · for Nothing Went Foom · [Bostrom 2026 · Optimal Timing for Superintelligence](https://nickbostrom.com/optimal.pdf) *(from a doom-side philosopher · he still backs a brief pause)* · [watch at 0:58](https://youtu.be/BmIz-atBA7U?t=58)
  > “Along one path (forgoing superintelligence), 170,000 people die every day of disease, aging, and other tragedies”

### How big, how fast?

| | Thus Spoke Compute · The End Of The Show<br>*leo · Imaginative Prompting* | Show Me the Chain · LGTM<br>*Sayash Kapoor · Degenerative Pixels* |
|---|---|---|

| **Misses** | Most of life adopts new tools slowly. | In code and maths, it's already racing. |

**The knot:** It can be both at once: racing in some fields and crawling in others, brilliant at one task and shaky at the next.

Receipts:

- **supports** · for Thus Spoke Compute · [Kwa et al. (METR) 2025 · Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499) *(software tasks, at 50% success)* · [watch at 1:39](https://youtu.be/BmIz-atBA7U?t=99)
